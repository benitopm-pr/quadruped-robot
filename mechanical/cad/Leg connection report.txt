Connection near the outer edge / upper part of the horn

(larger radius)

Mechanical effect

Long lever arm

Servo applies less force

But experiences more torque demand

Motion is faster, larger travel

Lower load capacity

Think: long wrench, easy to move, but you stall sooner.

The math (this is the key insight)
Fundamental equation

For a servo:

𝜏=𝐹⋅𝑟

Where:

τ = servo torque (N·m)

F = force in the linkage (N)

r = distance from servo center to linkage attachment (m)

Rearranged:

𝐹=𝜏/𝑟

What the servo “feels” in reverse (very important)

The leg pushes back on the servo.

The torque the servo must resist is:

𝜏𝑠𝑒𝑟𝑣𝑜=𝐹𝑙𝑒𝑔⋅𝑟
	​
So:

Outer attachment → larger 𝑟
r → servo torque rises quickly

Inner attachment → smaller 𝑟
r → servo survives higher loads

That’s why:

Outer holes stall servos faster

Inner holes feel “stronger” but slower


Why your pictured design uses that geometry
This is already a compound lever system.

So:

Small change in servo horn radius → big change at the foot

Designers usually:

Put the linkage not at the very edge

Not at the very center either

Somewhere in between to balance speed vs torque

Choose outer attachment if:

Robot is light

You want faster gait

You want larger stride

You accept less load margin
